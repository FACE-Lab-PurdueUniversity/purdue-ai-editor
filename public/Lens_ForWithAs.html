Engineering For / With / As People

A framework for naming your relationship to the people you're designing for — because "the user" is never just one relationship, and the difference between designing for, with, and as someone changes what you're allowed to assume.

The Big Idea

Your relationship to the people you design for isn't one thing.

Drawing on Fila et al. (2014) and Hynes & Swenson (2013), this framework splits "who is this for" into three distinct relational stances. You can design for a group you're not part of, with people as active collaborators, or as a way of designing from your own lived experience and values. Most real projects mix all three — naming which one you're in at any moment keeps you honest about what you actually know versus what you're assuming.

The Three Lenses

For, With, As.

Each stance carries a different obligation — and a different way you can go wrong.

01

For

Designing for people whose experience you don't share — a different age group, ability, background, or role than your own. The obligation is humility: don't assume you already know what they need.

What would I need to ask, not assume, about this person's experience?
02

With

Designing alongside people as active collaborators — teammates, co-designers, stakeholders with their own expertise. The obligation is shared authorship, not just consultation.

Whose expertise am I drawing on here, and are they actually shaping the decision?
03

As

Designing from your own identity, values, and lived experience — when you are, in some real sense, the person you're designing for. The obligation is honesty about what's personal versus universal.

What am I bringing from my own experience, and does it actually generalize?

Self-Check

Applying it to your own work.

Three questions to ask about anything you're building, at any stage.

For

Who am I designing for that I'm not, and what am I still assuming about their experience instead of asking?

With

Who's actually shaping this decision alongside me, and are they a real collaborator or just someone I checked in with?

As

What part of this comes from my own experience or values — and am I being honest about where that stops applying to someone else?

Source

Fila et al., "Engineering For, With, or As People?" (2014); Hynes & Swenson (2013).
